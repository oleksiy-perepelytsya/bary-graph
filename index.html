BaryGraph

BaryGraph turns relationships into searchable objects[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span).

It is a graph-and-vector architecture for finding useful conceptual coordinates: not only what is near a query, but what relation might connect two otherwise distant regions of a semantic space[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span).

The Idea in One Minute

In an ordinary knowledge graph, nodes are usually the first-class objects and edges are lightweight annotations[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span). BaryGraph makes the relation itself a first-class vector object, called a BaryEdge[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).

  • Two concepts are the children of a BaryEdge[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span).
  • The BaryEdge receives its own stored vector, which is a weighted, normalized composition of the child vectors and relation context[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).
  • BaryEdges can become children of higher-order relational objects, forming MetaBary triads[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).
  • Those triads can recurse upward, producing a forest of increasingly abstract relational patterns[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span).

The current Kaikki proof of concept contains approximately 6.66 million documents, including 1.74M sense nodes and 2.50M leaf-level BaryEdges[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span).

Field Reports & Explorations

The Boredom & Ritual Probe

A query explored whether boredom is a prerequisite for the invention of ritual, art, and mathematics[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span)[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span). The graph refused to let boredom be an absence, pairing it with overstimulation and "stuplime[span_11](start_span)[span_12](start_span)"[span_11](end_span)[span_12](end_span). It framed boredom as input without gradient[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span)[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span).

Between the concepts of ritualizing and ritualistic, the graph supplied bridges like "cadenced," "foot-tapping," "mensural," and "time step[span_15](start_span)[span_16](start_span)"[span_15](end_span)[span_16](end_span). This generated the hypothesis that undifferentiated time creates an impulse to impose measure, with ritual and number as the exports of that measure[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span)[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span). Additionally, a contradictory edge between an "inactigram" and memory/blank suggested that recorded inactivity is no longer blank[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span)[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span).

Designing for Uncertainty

Another probe asked how a community can treat uncertainty as a material for designing resilient rituals, institutions, or shared decisions[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span). A surprising bridge linked community and resistance with early-stage indirect microbiome modulation[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span).

This transposed into a practical design principle: under uncertainty, a community can run small, reversible interventions that change conditions rather than immediately committing to a permanent institution[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span). The graph acted as an associative instrument that provided a conceptual coordinate for models and users to operationalize[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span).

Resilience Through Redundancy

A final test spanned coral reefs, legal precedent, and error-correcting codes to explore resilience through redundancy[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span). The graph surfaced several senses of redundancy, including ecological functional overlap, duplicate components, repeated message structure, and legally removable surplus[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span).

A weak bridge involving the phrase "turn on" suggested that protective redundancy is often latent until a primary failure activates it[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span). The graph distinguished between useless repetition and diverse alternatives that fail independently, which can preserve a system's function[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span)[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span).
